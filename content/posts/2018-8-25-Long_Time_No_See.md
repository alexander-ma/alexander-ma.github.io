It's been awhile since I've written anything, and I feel like now's a good time for me to give an update as to what's been going on so far in my life. I'm on a plane for about four hours back to Austin, so buckle up, grab a beer (or some water I guess), and enjoy the ride.

The past two and a half months have been a pretty wild ride so far, and I've learn ***a lot*** about engineering, start-ups, running a business, and ultimately myself.

### What have you been up to?

Here's some things that I've taken an interest in so far:

- Ultimate frisbee
- Rock climbing
- Reading
- Photography
- Weightlifting
- Fashion
- Eating healthy
- Investing/Personal Finance
- Anime
- Programming
- Businesses, Start-ups

As you can see, this list has some pretty basic hobbies, sports, and activities. It's mind-blowing to me that I've been missing out on a lot of this stuff for a good chunk of my life, and it feels like now was the right time to finally explore them! If it weren't for being in Cambridge, I honestly have no clue what I'd be doing.

### How was your internship?

My internship has taught me a lot about the software engineering world. During my time here, I got to experience what it's like to be a full-stack software engineer using Ember.js and Ruby on Rails. I've never touched this sort of stuff before, so it was a good opportunity for me to learn how to make API calls, parse JSON objects, and play around with a rather large and sophistocated codebase.

The best part about my time at Ovation would have to be the brilliant and talented people that I get to work with every day. I'm not the best programmer, but I'm glad that everyone was very open to helping me grow my abilities as an engineer. Getting to talk to everyone at the office was great, and I'll definitely miss spending time having lunch together at the table in our kitchen eating some bomb vegan bowls from a local shop down the street (Life Alive, sponsor me pls).

### What's changed?

During this summer, I was able to spend time to self-reflect to figure myself out and who I wanted to be, what my goals are, and how I want to get there.

**Embracing Uncomfort** was something that I've always struggled with for the past few years. I still often find myself having troubles with being alone. Doing things that I want to do despite having no one to tag alongside me was a big step in the right direction for me. It was perfectly okay to be alone. A bit of myself was at ease not having people around me.

I also found myself **living spontaneously** and just doing things without worrying too much about what's going to happen next. A couple weeks ago my friends and I took a trip to New York City. As a small town boy from Amarillo, it's weird finding myself in such a big city. There wasn't much planning involved for the trip, and we just kind of went with the flow of things. There were definitely hiccups here and there, but that's okay.

I was okay with the random Puerto Rican parade and 30 minute delay from the 7 Train from Flushing. I was okay with spending an extra 4 hours in Manhattan from missing the bus back to Boston. I was okay with getting hustled out of $25 with a friend to attend a shitty 3 hour comedy show. The last-minute trip to the Big Apple was a great time, and was definitely a time well spent.


### Now let's get down to business.

I've been doing things wrong for the past 3 years. I have a year left to change it.

I've always been the type of person to dive headfirst into whatever I'm working on without thinking what I should do first to make this a lot easier on myself in the future. I tend to skim over the details of what I'm working on and what I'm trying to learn out of it.

Basically, I feel like a _copy-paste_ programmer.

I've skipped out on a lot of the details and tried forced myself to go through lots of hand-waving and head nods to get to where I am now. I am not proud of that **at all.** It's time for me to fix that. I need to be okay with constantly asking **HOW** things work, and **WHY** they happen to work that way. I need to train my mind to think differently in order to become a better problem solver, and I hope to man my own ship instead of staying on cruise control. Those 10,000 hours of work aren't going to happen on its own.

### So now what?

I'm not really sure what's next. I'll have to think about it some more. But in the meantime, I'd love to hear what people want me to write about.
